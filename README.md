# Temporal validity audit of LLM-extracted forward-looking signals

Anonymous repository accompanying the manuscript *"Are Forward-Looking Labels in
LLM-Extracted News Signals Temporally Valid? An Audit, an Intervention, and When
the Fix Pays."*

The repository holds the extraction and validation pipeline, the ground-truth
event table with its documented onset clocks, the prompt templates, the derived
measurement artifacts behind every table and figure in the paper, and the blind
human annotation worksheets.

## What this repository measures

A signal is a structured risk fragment extracted from one text. An event is the
disruption fact it is scored against. The audit asks one question: does a record
the pipeline labels `forward_looking` or `latent` actually precede the documented
onset of the event it names?

The reference clock is a public, dated onset, so the measurement closes without a
human gold set. Three quantities carry the result.

| Quantity | Definition | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Contamination `beta_0` | share of strictly post-onset reports labelled as warnings | `validation/metrics.py::compute_bscc` |
| Protocol precision | hits divided by forward signals in the pre-onset window | `validation/validate.py` |
| FWGS | lead-time and faithfulness weighted score (ex post, not a firing rule) | `validation/metrics.py` |

The break-even cost `c*` at which splitting the label schema repays the precursors
it discards is derived from the two schemas' own error counts; see
`results/MANIFEST.json` under `paired_slug` and `paired_body`.

## Onset provenance

Every lead-time figure is measured against the onset dates in
`validation/gt_events.json`, so those dates are the measurement instrument. Each
event carries:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `gt_onset_date` | the onset used as the reference clock |
| `onset_status` | `verified` for all seventeen events as of 2026-09-30 |
| `onset_evidence` | prose statement of what the source says |
| `onset_source` | registered source URL, where one exists |
| `onset_source_accessed` | ISO date on which that URL was checked |

`reports/onset_registry.md` renders these as a readable registry;
`analysis/onset_registry.py --check` audits them and `--latex` emits the
supplementary table. `reports/onsets_verify_20260930.md` is the raw evidence
trail assembled by `pipeline/verify_onsets.py --all` from Wikipedia.

Thirteen of the seventeen events have a registered source URL. The remaining four
rest on the primary authority named in their evidence field (a White House
announcement, a USTR Section 301 notice, a DGFT notification, and BLS CPI data)
rather than on a URL. No onset date was changed by the 2026-09-30 verification
pass.

## Layout

```
pipeline/       extraction, retrieval, matching, bootstrap and audit scripts
analysis/       metric aggregation, figure generation, manifest generation
validation/     ground truth (gt_events.json), date protocol, scoring functions
prompts/        the three extraction prompt templates (v1, v2 temporal, EDGAR)
results/        derived artifacts: MANIFEST.json, per-cell metrics, signal JSONL
data/           ground-truth-facing inputs (see "Data included" below)
reports/        onset provenance notes
```

`results/MANIFEST.json` is the single consolidated record of the reported
numbers. It is generated by `analysis/results_manifest.py` and every value quoted
in the manuscript is read from it rather than typed by hand.

## Data excluded, and why

The article bodies fetched from publisher sites and web archives
(`data/by_event_body/`, `data/body_fetch/`) are **not** included. The underlying
text is third-party copyright content and is not redistributed here or in the
manuscript.

Everything needed to reconstitute the corpus is included: `data/by_event/` gives
the URL, slug and date of every item in the keyword pool, so the bodies can be
re-fetched from the publishers or from the public archives named in the paper.
The retrieval stage is `pipeline/fetch_background.py` for GDELT item collection
and `pipeline/free_archives.py` plus the `fetch_bodies_pass*.py` scripts for body
recovery.

Note that the full-text results in the paper are a *subset* of the corpus, since
the body fetch succeeded for 92.5% of rows. Re-running on a different day will
therefore reproduce the slug-side numbers exactly and the body-side numbers only
approximately, because archive availability drifts.

### Read this before regenerating `results/MANIFEST.json`

Excluding the article bodies has one consequence that is **not** signalled by an
error, so it is called out here.

`FWGS` contains a faithfulness term `g_i`, the share of a signal's
`trigger_phrases` that appear verbatim in the source text. The source text for the
full-text cells lives in `data/by_event_body/`, which is withheld. In its absence:

1. `pipeline/recompute_cell.py::load_articles` returns an empty mapping,
2. `faithfulness()` hits its `if not text: return 0.0` branch, and
3. the full-text `FWGS` values come out **negative** instead of near 0.4.

Measured on this snapshot: regenerating the manifest without the body corpus
prints `body_single_field FWGS -0.1524` and `body_clock_split FWGS -0.1311`,
against the reported `0.396` and `0.415`. Nothing raises; the script reports
success.

The two `slug` cells are unaffected, because their source text is the URL slug
and is included in `data/by_event/`. They regenerate to `0.4189` and `0.4990`,
matching the reported `0.419` and `0.499`.

So:

* the reported full-text `FWGS` values are reproducible only with the body corpus
  restored, which is why the committed `results/MANIFEST.json` records them;
* the committed `results/MANIFEST.json` is the reference copy and should not be
  overwritten with a manifest regenerated from the withheld-body snapshot;
* if you restore the bodies and regenerate, compare the `body_*` `fwgs` fields
  against the committed values before trusting anything downstream of them.

The extraction counts, precisions, false-alarm counts and break-even costs for
the full-text cells are all reproducible without the bodies, since they depend
only on the signal files and the ground truth.

## Reproducing the reported numbers

Python 3.11 or later. Install the dependencies:

```
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

Regenerate the consolidated manifest, then verify any claim against it:

```
python analysis/results_manifest.py     # writes results/MANIFEST.json
python pipeline/stats_canonical.py      # bootstrap intervals, beta_0, cost ratios
python analysis/postevent_breakdown.py  # the post-event negative-control pool
python analysis/leadtime_inflation.py   # mean propagation lag and phantom lead
```

All scripts are standalone and take no arguments; each prints a table to stdout
and writes a JSON artifact under `results/`.

**Caveat on the first command.** `analysis/results_manifest.py` overwrites
`results/MANIFEST.json`. Run it only after reading the note on the faithfulness
term in "Data excluded, and why" above: without the article bodies it silently
produces wrong full-text `FWGS` values. To inspect the reported manifest, read the
committed copy rather than regenerating it.

The extraction stages require a running OpenAI-compatible LLM endpoint (the paper
used a local `llama.cpp` server hosting `qwen3.8-27b` at `temperature=0`). Those
stages are not needed to reproduce the reported numbers, because the extracted
signals are already in `results/`. They are included so that the pipeline can be
re-run against a different model or corpus.

## Human annotation

Two independent annotators blind-labelled the retrieval-audit sample and the
forward-signal spot-check; disagreements were adjudicated by a third party. The
worksheets, the per-annotator label files and the adjudicated merges are in
`data/annotation/`. The agreement statistics reported in the paper (Cohen's
kappa of 0.756 and 0.655) are computed by `analysis/annotation_agreement.py`.

## Notes on scope

- The instrument is retrospective. It scores a corpus whose onsets are already
  known, so it can rank past warnings but cannot fire a live system.
- Full extraction recall is not measured. It would require labelling the
  non-firing pool, which is a different question from the one the spot-check
  asks, namely whether the signals a decision would rest on are genuine.
- `run_pipeline.py` is the original end-to-end orchestrator. It was written for
  the development layout and prints paths relative to its own parent directory,
  which only affects the printed log, not the artifacts it writes.
